Let me tell you the ways of science. What is scientific method? Science deals with truth. The central question is this. How do you know a claim is true or false? If I say that 2 massive objects attract each other (gravity), how do I know that is true or false? The answer is that you test the claim via experiment, the results we get we call evidence. Observation + experimentation = empiricism. If it produces the same results again and again independently, we can be reasonably sure that the claim is true. New evidence can emerge that can prove our claim wrong, in which case we can refine the claim and test again. This quite literally happened with gravity. Newton said gravity is a force, which held true for many many years. Einstein said it was curvature in space time. The practical difference only started mattering when our space observations became more precise.

Another important feature of science is falsifiability. You have a claim. The whole world will muster all the resources it can and attack your claim. The more attacks your claim survives, the more confidence we can have in it. 

What does this have to do with stonks and monies? Well mathematics and thus statistics are languages of science. Thus languages of nature itself. If you want to measure reality, you can't do it without mathematics, well you can, early science was all done intuitively, but measurements became important enough that a lot of natural science was translated into mathematics. Einstein himself joked: "Since the mathematicians have invaded the theory of relativity, I do not understand it myself anymore."

This applies to the reality we call financial markets. I would argue that financial market study is one of the more difficult branches of science. Numbers show up on charts that have a basis in psychological perceptions of many humans. How do you measure that? You cannot come up with a perfect model for that, even if you have perfect models for economics and everything else, which we really don't. You can't even begin to identify all the variables that go into shaping these charts. Times change, and so do markets. Demographics change, and so do markets. Climate changes, and so do markets. 

You can somewhat make the same claims regarding thermodynamics. We can study macroscopic properties of matter, like heat and pressure, if we have a perfect model of all microscopic properties of that matter. But it is not so. We don't need to do it. We can measure temperature by how much mercury expands. Statistical mechanics is a broader study that allows us to study macroscopic properties of matter without modeling microscopic properties to perfection. 

The same holds true for financial markets. We can apply scientific principles to financial markets through statistical modelling. Formulate hypotheses, conduct experiments, record observations, and evaluate how much confidence, if any, we can place in our claims. We cannot perfectly measure all microscopic forces at play in markets. Statistics is a means to study macroscopic properties. Financial markets cannot be modeled perfectly.
