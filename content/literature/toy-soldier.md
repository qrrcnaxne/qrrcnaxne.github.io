There was once a broken soldier. Lying around the dark alley, near those huge dustbin. He was sitting down with his head hanging over his shoulder. The stains over his cheeks and clothes made him look dirty. His eyes were droopy. They told a story.

He was mighty once. Fought in great wars under great leaders. Running in the flat fields. Charging on the enemy castles. Destroying renowned enemies. He has a gun with him. Longer than his torso and head combined. It was clutched to his belt. Pointing up. Like it was about fire and give a signal. But it stayed silent. What did it say?

Wars are fought between leaders. It is an ideological battle. An intellectual debate between philosophers. How should a country be ruled? What kind of a country would you like to live in? Rulers followed them. Tolstoy believed that rulers were a manifestation of the people's will. The destiny of such a war was going to decide the superior ideology. A war for land and resources. A war for monopoly. A war for power.

What was the soldier's part in this? He was made to go through a lot of processing and molding and coloring to be who he was. That became his identity.

The soldier fought to protect his people. And his family. There was no ideology. No politics involved. It objectively made sense without getting complicated things involved. It was pure. Untainted by the waters of immorality. When he charged, his resolve was rock solid. He couldn't charge with a doubt in his mind.
