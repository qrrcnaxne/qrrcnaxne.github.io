I was supposed to be seeing a box. But what was it? It was nothing like I pictured. A box is supposed to have lines and planes and dots. They’re supposed to be connected to each other. But they weren’t. Was it even a box? I knew I wasn’t dreaming. I was supposed to be here. I could see the colorful lines of the box; they had a very peculiar color against the white background; probably the edges. I couldn’t make out all the edges though, I didn’t know if they were even there, just the ones that were directly in front of me were visible.

I was in a room. One of those white rooms you’re supposed to be in when you’re dead. It was so white I couldn’t see the corners of the room. I could barely make out walls too.

I was told I was special.

I did not remember how I ended up here or how I knew these things.

It seemed like a box because it could contain. I did not know what was in it, or if something can be put in it. It was transparent. The lines were emitting a colorful rainbow like light. I could see it on all sides; like a thin membrane.

I started seeing something inside the box. It did contain something after all. I couldn’t figure out what it was; it glowed brightly. I couldn’t see it before. Now I could. I wasn’t aware of the reality. How fast or slow was the time passing? I could feel my mind in its place, not the rest of the body. I was floating, but there was gravity, the box was floating but it was kept on a platform; I couldn’t see or feel the platform, but it was there. I didn’t know how I knew this. Everything was floating but not the way did it in space. There was friction because of which things could stop or slow down and because of which I could move. I couldn’t reach the box. I wanted to try. But putting in the efforts seemed futile. It was too far away. It seemed as if, if I try to reach it, it would move away and wouldn’t come back. I didn’t wanna chase it away. I stood still. Watching.

The light from the box was very strange. Not the ordinary sunlight or even the artificial lights that we create in our homes. I kept looking and the light kept on glowing brighter. The light seemed white with a yellow afterglow, like it was growing stale. It was losing something the further it came. I was afraid it would blind me. It didn’t. My eyes felt no pain. They usually did after looking at something so bright. Eventually, the light stopped growing and became more yellow than before. It was losing more than it could produce, so it figured it should stop. The place I was in was illuminated completely. I could see the whiteness of the walls, they were really bright. I couldn’t figure out if it was this light or it was some other light.

The uncertainty of the room, the invisibility of the box, the object in the light, all made me feel that I couldn’t see beyond what I was supposed to see. This was its nature perhaps. The object was why I had come here in the first place. What was the yellow light? Was it telling me that I shouldn’t take the object? I was paranoid. This place wasn’t for me. This place didn’t want me here. This place was something I wasn’t supposed to know, much less be in.

I had a dark brown color shirt with grayish design on it and a dark blue color pant which had a very rough texture; like a sand paper; except they weren’t grains but long continuous protruding lines. Usually I pick up any clothes which don’t look ugly. I didn’t have anything extra on, like a watch, or cap or a sack. I remember I had a sack.

How could I take the object, I couldn’t even reach it.

The elders had told me I would be made aware of things as I needed them.

I needed to know, or perhaps I wanted to know. I don’t know, how could I know?

Perhaps that’s how the light appeared. I thought I was like a puppet controlled by someone or something other than me. I could only think what they wanted me to think; I could only know what they wanted me to know. Was it the object in the box that was controlling me? If that was the case, why did it allow me to question? Did it want me to question so it could give me the relevant answer when I come to the proper question? I looked at the object. I felt nothing. Perhaps this wasn’t the right question. The object didn’t interact with me like I wanted it to. It showed no sign of wanting to get out of the box. It wasn’t trying. Or it couldn’t try? Did it want me to get it out of there? Why did it show itself? Perhaps the box was a prison. I waited for some signal. I didn’t get any. Perhaps I had a choice. I was here to retrieve it after all. I should get it. I suddenly felt a sense of intense risk. Of course there was a risk. I had to take it anyway.
